# Code of Conduct

Mutual aid works better when people can actually stand being in the room together.

This project welcomes people with different backgrounds, identities, abilities, experience levels, and relationships to tech. You do not need to be a developer, policy expert, nonprofit professional, or Very Serious GitHub Person to contribute something useful.

## The basic rule

**Treat people like people.**

That means:

- disagree with ideas without treating the person like an enemy;
- explain things without talking down to people;
- make room for people who communicate differently than you do;
- do not harass, threaten, stalk, dox, or deliberately humiliate anyone;
- do not use slurs or dehumanizing language;
- do not publish somebody else's private information;
- do not turn a resource-maintenance discussion into an interrogation of the person who needed the resource;
- do not use this project to recruit for hate groups, target vulnerable people, or make the community less safe.

## We care more about impact than cleverness

A joke that makes the project warmer is great.

A joke that makes somebody wonder whether they're welcome here is not particularly useful to us.

If somebody tells you that something landed badly, you do not have to agree with every interpretation of your intent. You do need to stop and deal with the actual effect on the work and the people doing it.

## Criticism is allowed

You can say:

- a source is wrong;
- an idea will not work;
- a page is confusing;
- a process is inaccessible;
- a decision should be reconsidered.

Please explain why.

"That's a terrible idea" is not nearly as useful as "That puts the phone number behind three clicks, which is a problem on a crisis page."

## Privacy and safety come first

This repository is public.

Do not post private information about people seeking mutual aid, survivors, children, organizers, or anyone else who has not intentionally made that information public.

See [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md) for the full version.

## If something goes wrong

If a conversation gets hostile, unsafe, discriminatory, or wildly off the rails, maintainers may edit, hide, lock, or remove content and may limit someone's participation.

The goal is not to win an argument about rules. The goal is to keep the project useful and safe enough for people to keep doing the work.

## One last thing

We are building resources for people who may be having a very bad day.

A little patience goes a long way.
